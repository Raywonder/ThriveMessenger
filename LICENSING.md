# Thrive Messenger licensing and source-availability policy

Thrive Messenger is a downstream project based on MIT-licensed upstream work. The
upstream copyright and permission notice must remain available with every copy or
substantial portion of that upstream-derived software.

## What this means

- Code and assets received under the MIT License remain usable under that license.
- A later policy change cannot withdraw rights already granted for published copies.
- Divine Creations may keep new, independently owned components proprietary when
  those components are clearly identified and no upstream or third-party license
  requires their source to be published.
- A public repository should not be described as wholly closed source while its
  source is publicly available and contains MIT-licensed upstream material.
- Third-party packages, media, fonts, sounds, and other assets retain their own
  licenses and notices.

Before changing repository visibility, distribution terms, or license wording,
inventory the upstream-derived and independently owned portions and obtain legal
review where ownership is uncertain.

## Contributions

External contributions are accepted only when a maintainer has opened or invited a
pull request. Submitting a contribution does not change the license of upstream or
third-party material. The contribution terms must be stated in the pull request and
accepted by a maintainer before merge.

This document records project policy; it is not legal advice and does not replace
the license notices that apply to particular files or dependencies.
